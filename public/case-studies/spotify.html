The decision in one sentence

Improve the first listen before changing the price.

Keep the existing 12 monthly hours in eligible Premium plans. Test a clearer path from discovering a book to returning for the next chapter.

Why this direction?
Spotify already offers audiobook listening in these markets. The opportunity is adoption and profitable growth.

12-hour offer: public Spotify market pages. Proposed discovery changes and all demo content: portfolio concept.

01 / Problem statement

Having access is only the beginning.

A new listener may not know which audiobook to choose, whether it is included, or how the listening allowance works. That uncertainty could stop them before their first chapter.

Business problem: included listening costs Spotify money. It needs to help people join Premium, stay longer or buy extra hours to justify that cost.

Customer barriers are hypotheses to validate. No interviews or Spotify internal results are claimed.

02 / User story

Make my first book an easy choice.

As a Spotify listener who is new to audiobooks, I want to find a book that fits my interests and understand what my plan includes, so I can try listening with confidence.

Success means: I find a relevant book, know my available hours, start listening and can easily return.

Primary audience: eligible Premium listeners new to audiobooks. Free-to-Premium conversion is a secondary business test.

03 / Experience and interactive screens

One useful recommendation. One clear next step.

The proposed first-listen journey sits inside Spotify. It asks about book interests, shows a relevant recommendation and explains included hours before playback.

  1. Discover: see the audiobook benefit in Premium.
  2. Choose: pick interests and review a suggested book.
  3. Listen: understand the allowance and start.
  4. Return: continue from the last chapter.

Interactive concept with a fictional book. English presentation for the portfolio; production would use local languages. No audio, payment or account changes.

User action → feedback statements

These statements explain what the listener does and what the product shows in response.

  • Open audiobook discovery → see what the current plan includes.
  • Select book interests → see a recommendation reflecting that choice.
  • Open the book → see its duration, inclusion status and available hours.
  • Start or pause → see the current listening state.
  • Return later → see a clear continue-listening option.
  • Use all included hours → see the renewal date and optional paid continuation; never an automatic charge.

04 / Market choice and competition

Brazil first. Mexico next. Poland third.

Prioritize learning and spending in this order. All three have public Spotify pages describing 12 included hours on eligible Premium plans; these are existing-offer growth opportunities.

MarketWhy prioritize it?What must be proven?
1. BrazilA useful first test against affordable specialist subscriptions, including Audible, Storytel and Ubook.Can relevant Portuguese books make the existing bundle more valuable?
2. MexicoBookwire reports growing Mexican share in its Spanish-language audiobook analysis. Skeelo already competes through partner bundles.Can Spotify make choosing a first book easier than competing included-access offers?
3. PolandStrong specialist alternatives: Storytel, Audioteka and Empik Go.Can convenience win newcomers without copying unlimited specialist plans?

Proposed ranking, based on demand signals, bundle fit, competition and learning value. Rights costs and Spotify’s local eligible audience could change the order.

What competitors teach us

Audible: local plans differ. In Brazil its advertised R$19.90 monthly subscription includes unlimited listening to a defined catalog; other titles cost extra. Spotify must make inclusion clear.

Storytel: specialist subscriptions in all three markets show why catalog relevance matters. Spotify’s advantage to test is convenience within an existing audio habit.

Local providers: Ubook tests local relevance in Brazil; Skeelo tests distribution in Mexico; Audioteka and Empik Go test specialist value in Poland. A larger global catalog alone is not a winning argument.

06 / Research and rollout

Start small and learn.

Interview 10–12 new audiobook users per market. Ask what they listen to today, when a book might fit and what stops them. Test the screens with five people per market before a limited Brazil pilot.

Compare the proposed journey with today’s experience. Keep existing benefits in both groups. If understanding, return listening and business value improve, adapt the experience for Mexico, then Poland.

Proposed research plan. A small usability study finds problems; it does not prove market demand.

07 / Success measures

Did it help—and was it worth it?

First listen: more exposed users reach 20 minutes of listening.
Habit: more return during the next 30 days.
Business value: extra earnings exceed extra costs.
Guardrails: monitor cancellations, billing complaints and music experience.

Proposed pilot goals: 15% relative improvement in first listens, at least 90% correct allowance understanding and positive extra earnings after costs. These are targets, not results.

08 / Simple business case

More listening must also create value.

Compare what the new journey adds against what would have happened anyway. Do not count every existing Premium payment as new audiobook revenue.

All figures are illustrative assumptions, not Spotify forecasts or contract estimates. Earnings are after ordinary service costs; costs include only the extra effect of this proposal. USD allows a simple comparison; real decisions need local currencies and setup costs.

How I would explain and validate the numbers

In the expected example, 200 extra paying-customer months × $10 left after ordinary service costs = $2,000. Another 500 paid extra-hour purchases × $2 left after their costs = $1,000. Together that is $3,000.

Subtract $1,600 of extra bundled listening costs and $400 of support and lost full-price sales: $1,000 remains. Every input is an assumption to replace with pilot evidence. New and retained customer counts must not overlap.

If setup costs were $12,000 and the same $1,000 surplus continued every month, recovering setup costs would take 12 months. A slower start makes it longer. Stop or change the proposal if it only increases listening costs.

09 / Risks and tradeoffs

Three things could change the decision.

The right books may be missing.

Check customer-requested local titles and secure country-specific listening rights before promotion. Global catalog size does not guarantee local relevance.

Included listening may replace paid sales.

Track lost purchases and add-on income alongside gains. Existing customers listening more is useful only if the overall business improves.

Hours may feel confusing or restrictive.

Show the allowance early and offer a fair choice at the limit. Avoid claiming that every book can be finished within the monthly hours.

The tradeoff: start with clearer discovery and existing prices. This is easier to test, but may create less immediate revenue than a price increase.
